Project 1: Image Alignment using Pyramid Structures

CS180 Project 1 - Fall 2026

By: Jessica Nguyen

Overview

Sergei Prokudin-Gorskii, a photographer working in the early 1900s, pioneered color photography in the Russian Empire at a time when color printing technology didn't yet exist. His technique involved photographing each scene three separate times, using red, green, and blue filters respectively, and recording all three exposures on a single glass plate.

The Library of Congress now preserves these digitized negatives, which are arranged with the three color separations stacked vertically — blue at the top, green in the middle, and red at the bottom. This project implements image alignment techniques to reconstruct color images from digitized glass plate photographs.

Alignment Methods Implemented:

  • Single-Scale: Direct exhaustive search over shift offsets
  • Multi-Scale: Image pyramid with NCC/L2 scoring

Single-Scale Alignment

Approach

For the single-scale alignment, I implemented an exhaustive search method that looked through a range of [-15, 15] pixels in both x and y directions for the green and red channels relative to the blue channel. The alignment was scored using the L2 norm (sum of squared differences) to find the best offsets that minimized the difference between channels.

The reason I chose the L2 norm is that it is simple to compute and effective for images with consistent lighting and contrast. This allowed for a fast computation time. One of the challenges I faced was the problem with the edges of the images, since the edges of the images were dark boarders, the search would sometimes find offsets that aligned the edges of the images, but not the actual content of the images. So I decided to crop the images by 10% on each side to avoid this issue.

Multi-Scale Alignment (Image Pyramid)

Approach

For better performance on larger images, I implemented a multi-scale approach using image pyramids. The algorithm:

  • Builds Gaussian pyramids for each channel
  • Starts alignment at the coarsest scale
  • Progressively refines alignment at finer scales
  • Uses the coarse alignment as initialization for fine alignment

This approach significantly speeds up computation for large images while maintaining alignment quality.

Key Findings

  • Pyramid-based alignment provides significant speedup over single-scale methods
  • Cropping edges by ~10% prevents edge artifacts in alignment
  • The L2 norm is effective for this task but can be sensitive to lighting variations
  • Multi-scale approach handles images of varying sizes effectively
